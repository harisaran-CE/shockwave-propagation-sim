How this model works

Each car, motorcycle, auto-rickshaw, bus, and truck is simulated separately. Each physical lane has four marked paths for choosing where to travel; vehicles still need enough room for their actual width and length, so a marked path is not a promise that a vehicle can fit beside another one. Moves between paths take about 2.4 seconds and are checked for a clear route. Every vehicle follows the same snapshot of traffic each step, then advances. A leader never brakes, changes lane, or moves backward just to make room for a follower; only a vehicle behind slower traffic can request a pass. Drivers brake for traffic ahead and keep a gap when they can. They look at least 220 m ahead for a lane closure and merge sooner when front and rear gaps are safe; they wait rather than force an unsafe merge. A driver must be closing on slower traffic to pass and waits before returning to the old lane.

A lane closure acts as a stopped obstacle in that lane. A full closure blocks both lanes. Vehicles slow down and queue; lane changes can move them around a single-lane restriction when there is room.

Drag the barrier marker directly on the animated road, or edit its distance. Moving it does not reset the run; vehicles already past it keep moving. Vehicles accelerate normally when the barrier is well ahead, then brake within a safe stopping distance. Vehicle lengths keep the road scale, with clearer, larger shapes that do not shrink when traffic gets close.

Readouts include mean speed in km/h, density in vehicles per kilometre per lane, exiting and queued vehicle counts, overtakes, and lane changes. Contact prevention is reported separately from barrier queues.

Vehicles reaching the 500 m downstream end are counted as exited and do not stop the simulation. The run stops at its selected time, or when congestion keeps new vehicles from entering at the 0 m upstream end.

These are simplified, uncalibrated driving rules. Results are for exploration, not road-safety or engineering decisions.
